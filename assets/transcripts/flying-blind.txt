Story: Flying Blind
Narrator: Captain James Osei
Duration: 18 minutes 24 seconds
Published: March 2025
==============================================

The altimeter was spinning. I remember that clearly — not because it was unusual, but because of how slowly it moved, like it had something important to say. Outside the cockpit there was nothing. No horizon. No lights below. Just grey pressing in from every direction, the kind of dark that swallows you whole if you let it.

Tower had gone silent twenty minutes earlier. My co-pilot, Marcus, was working the radios but getting nothing back — just static and the sound of rain hammering against the fuselage. We were somewhere over the North Atlantic. Eleven thousand feet, according to the instruments. I wasn't sure I believed them anymore.

I looked at Marcus. He looked at me. Neither of us said a word for ten seconds. That silence was the loudest thing I have ever heard inside a cockpit. Then he said, quietly, "What do you want to do?" And I realised that no one was going to answer that question except me.

I had trained for instrument failure. Every pilot does. But training happens in a simulator, in a building, with an instructor who already knows the answer. This was different. This was seven tonnes of metal and forty-two passengers, and the only person who could bring them home was the one holding the yoke.

I went back to basics. Attitude. Altitude. Airspeed. The three things your body wants to abandon when it can't see and the instruments are lying. I held the wings level by feel and started to climb, slowly, because height is time and time is options. Marcus got a partial signal on the secondary radio — just enough to get a bearing.

Forty minutes later we broke through the clouds. There were lights below. I have landed that aircraft three thousand times. I have never felt the wheels touch down the way I felt them that night.
